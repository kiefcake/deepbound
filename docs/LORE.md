# Deepbound: the lore

A living document. It is written to fit the game as it is built: every name here is already in
the game (floors, bosses, enemies, relics, systems), and the lore explains why it works the way
it does. Where a system has no story yet, it gets one here before it gets flavour text in game.

---

## The short version

Beneath the barony of Ravenhold there was once a holy city, **Cathedra**, built downward instead
of up. Its people believed that the closer you were buried to God, the sooner you would wake on
the Last Morning. So they dug: an ossuary for the common dead, flooded vaults for the merchants,
a throne for the king who ruled the city of the buried, and at the very bottom a black cathedral
where the last Cardinal sang the dead to sleep.

The singing stopped. Nobody remembers when. The dead did not wake on the Last Morning; they woke
early, and wrong, and they have been climbing ever since.

Castle Ravenhold sits on the only shaft that still leads down. Its people keep one lantern lit at
the top of it, **the Last Lantern**, so that whoever goes down can find the way back. You are one
of the people who go down.

## The world above

### Castle Ravenhold
A round keep with a curtain wall and six towers, built over the shaft three hundred years ago by
the first Baron Ravenhold to seal the dead in. It did not seal them in. Now it is the last town
on the road: a gatehouse, a carriage that runs on rails down the shaft, a road of braziers, a few
stalls run by people with nowhere better to be, and a gambling tent run by people with nowhere
else at all.

- **The merchant**, Silas Crane, sells potions and plate. He does not ask where your gold came from, because
  he knows.
- **The blacksmith's anvil** is older than the castle. It was carried up out of the deep by the
  first expedition that came back. Embers from below are the only thing that will heat it, and
  the only thing it will temper is a weapon that has already been down there.
- **The tailor** dyes cloaks for those with a name worth wearing. Renown is spoken of at camp the
  way gold is spoken of in the dungeon.
- **The Omen Stone** is a standing stone the castle was built around. Turn it, and the deep turns
  with it.
- **The bounty board** carries contracts nailed up by people who will never go down themselves.
- **The shrine** grants a blessing for a single descent. The priest who tends it will not say
  whose blessing it is.
- **The gambling tent** holds the bones game, the Bone Reels (a cabinet salvaged from the Drowned
  Vaults, whose reels still turn up skulls more often than crowns), and **Deadman's Hand**, a card
  table where the dealer is a skeleton who walked up out of the shaft one night, sat down, and has
  not lost a hand since. Nobody has tried to send it back.
- **The cat.** No one owns the cat. The cat has been at Ravenhold longer than anyone can remember,
  and it is the only living thing that has been down the shaft and come back unchanged. It brings
  you coins sometimes. Do not ask where it finds them. It has opinions about being picked up. Ask first (pet it), and it will smile and leave you a
  whisker, which delvers swear makes the dead miss. Do not ask, and it will make its opinion known
  all down your front, and the smell follows you the whole descent.

### The people of Ravenhold (in game: they talk when you walk past)
- **Silas Crane, the merchant.** A stooped peddler who has not taken his hood off since the war;
  nobody remembers which war, least of all Silas. Sells potions he will not explain, refuses to
  touch Seal fragments, and has opinions about how you smell after the cat has had its say.
- **Hobb Ironside, the smith.** Keeper of the anvil that came up with the first expedition. It
  only takes embers from below; he tried good coal once and it spat it back at him. His
  grandfather swore the old Baron would hold the first door until the end of days.
- **Brother Aldric, the shrine priest.** Kind, evasive about whose blessing he gives, and the
  only person in Ravenhold who sings for the dead. Has fed the cat every day for nine years and
  has never been given a whisker.
- **Mistress Vey, the tailor.** Takes Renown, never gold, because gold is for people with no
  taste. Once dyed a ghoul. It was much improved.
- **Corwin, the carriage driver.** Forty years on the seat and never seen the bottom; keeps his
  eyes shut on the way down. Counts the times he has driven the carriage home empty, and talks to
  the seats now.
- **Mister Bones, the dealer.** The skeleton at Deadman's Hand. It walked up out of the shaft one
  night and sat down, and it has been shuffling ever since. It never speaks. If anyone ever brings
  the Unsung to its end, they say it will stop shuffling, and bow.

### The Last Lantern and the waystones
The lantern at the top of the shaft burns with a flame that was lit from the Cardinal's own
censer before the singing stopped. Every expedition that goes down carries that light with it,
and where they make camp they set it into a **waystone**, a carved standing stone that holds the
light in the dark.

A waystone does three things, and every delver learns them:

1. It remembers the way home. That is why the carriage can find you, and why the light can take
   you down (the **descent**: a column of lantern-light that lifts you from the castle and sets you
   down at the waystone below).
2. It keeps a door open after a guardian falls. When a floor's master dies, the lantern-light it
   was holding back floods out as **a portal**, and it waits. It will wait as long as you need. It
   only takes you home when everyone who came down together steps into it together.
3. It gives back what the dark took. That is the oldest rule, and the one that matters most.

### Souls and the soul skull
Down there, death is slow. A delver who falls does not die at once; the lantern-light they carry
holds them, kneeling, for a while (**downed**). A friend's hand on their shoulder can pull them
back up. If no one comes, the light goes out of them and settles in the bone of their skull, and
what is left is a **soul skull**: violet, faintly warm, heavier than it looks.

Carried to a waystone, the skull is given back. The stone has never yet refused one. Nobody knows
whether it could.

The bled-out can still speak, a little. Only those standing close can hear them.

## The world below

### The four floors

**1. The Ossuary.** Where Cathedra put its common dead: miles of bone stacked in niches, chapels
built of skulls, corridors paved with the people who dug them. The bones here do not stay down.
A skeleton that is knocked apart drags itself back together unless someone grinds the pile under
their heel. Its guardian is **the Gravewarden**.

**2. The Drowned Vaults.** The merchant quarter, where the wealthy were buried with their wealth.
The river found it a hundred years after the singing stopped. The water rises and falls like a
tide that has forgotten the moon, and what the vaults hold now is whatever the water has not
taken. Its master is **the Tide Lich**.

**3. The Hollow Throne.** The palace of the city of the buried, where the King of the Dead held
court over people who could no longer leave. The soul lanterns that lit his hall still burn with
the trapped light of his courtiers; break one, and the light goes to whoever stands nearest.
It is ruled by **Ossric, the Hollow King**.

**4. The Black Cathedral.** The bottom. The church where the Cardinal sang the dead to sleep every
night for four hundred years. Its bell still tolls on the hour, and the toll still knocks the
living off their feet. Its voice is **the Pale Cardinal**.

### The guardians

**The Gravewarden, Keeper of the First Door.**
The first Baron Ravenhold did not only build a castle over the shaft. He went down it. He took an
oath at the first door of the Ossuary that nothing would pass him, and nothing has: not the dead
coming up, and not the living going down. He has stood there so long that the stone has grown
into his shield. When the shield is finally thrown aside he fights faster, because he no longer
remembers what he was guarding, only that he must.

> *"You knock at the first door. Now it knocks back."*

**The Tide Lich, Who Drowned the Vaults.**
A banker of the Drowned Vaults who would not be buried without his ledgers, and would not be
parted from them when the river came. He learned the water's language in the dark. He speaks it
fluently now. He hoards drowned names the way he once hoarded coin, and when he is wounded he
sinks into the deep of his own vault and lets the water do the fighting.

> *"The water remembers every drowned name. Yours is next."*

**Ossric, the Hollow King, Sovereign of the Empty Throne.**
The last king of Cathedra, crowned in the city of the buried by a Cardinal who promised him he
would rule until the Last Morning. He has ruled. His Royal Guard still kneel when he calls them,
and his crown still passes judgement on one of his subjects at a time. The trick, which every
court learns, is that judgement shared among many weighs less on each.

> *"I was a king before your gods were born!"*

**The Pale Cardinal, Last Voice of the Black Cathedral.**
The one who sang. For four hundred years the Cardinal's hymn kept every dead thing in Cathedra
asleep, and every night the hymn cost a little more of the Cardinal. When the last of the Cardinal
was spent the hymn did not stop. It changed. The choir of wraiths that answers it now is made of
everyone the Cardinal ever sang to sleep. The Cardinal is no longer singing them to rest. The
Cardinal is singing them to wake.

> *"Kneel. Kneel, and burn."*

*(Design note: the Cardinal is a bare skull under a two-peaked mitre, never a hood over a face.)*

### The dead

- **Skeletons** are Cathedra's common dead, still holding the tools of whoever they were.
  Shieldbearers were the city watch; their tower shields carry the arms of a city no one remembers.
- **Archers** were the wall-guard of the Ossuary chapels and still hold the arrow-slits.
- **Ghouls** are the dead who woke hungry. They hunt in pairs because they were buried in pairs.
- **Bone Brutes** are what happens when an ossuary's bones are stacked carelessly: several dead,
  one body, no agreement about which way to walk.
- **Necromancers** were Cathedra's undertakers. They still do the work of putting the dead back on
  their feet. They simply no longer wait for the Last Morning.
- **Wraiths** are the Cardinal's choir: souls that never settled into bone, only half there until
  something pins them down.
- **Gargoyles** were carved to watch over the dead and took the job too literally.
- **Mimics** are Cathedra's greed given teeth. The Drowned Vault merchants buried their coffers
  with wards against thieves. The wards are still working.
- **Bone Hoarders** carry off whatever gold the dead dropped, out of habit.
- **Plaguebearers** died of the sickness that emptied the upper city, and still carry it.
  **Frost Wights** are the dead of the year the river froze. **Hexers** were the witches the
  Cardinal buried at the bottom so their curses would sink, but curses do not sink.
- **The Mourners** are the statues that stood over the graves of the important dead, carved
  weeping. They do not move while anyone is looking at them. Nobody has ever seen one move.
  Everyone who has been to the Drowned Vaults has seen one closer than it was a moment ago.

### The light, and what it buys
- **Embers** are what is left of a dead thing's light when it falls apart. The anvil eats them.
- **Sigils** are pressed embers that remember a shape, and teach it to the hand that holds them.
- **Runes** are older than Cathedra. The city found them already carved in the rock it dug into.
- **Relics** are the belongings of saints of Cathedra, each holding one saint's particular
  obsession (the Phoenix Feather would not let its saint stay dead; the Vampiric Fang would not
  let its saint stay hungry).
- **Reliquaries** are the gilded boxes those saints' remains were carried in. They still sing the
  hymn they were sealed with, very quietly, and the dead still come to the sound. Carried home, a
  reliquary is worth a small fortune. The castle buys every one and none of them are ever seen
  again.
- **Potions** are brewed at Ravenhold from things that should not be brewed. Health potions are
  red because of what is in them. Mana potions are blue because of what is in them. Frost flasks
  are bottled river from the year it froze. Holy water is drawn from the font of the shrine, and
  the priest will not say from where the font is fed. There is no natural healing below; the
  lantern-light keeps you alive, but it does not mend you.
- **Gold** down there is the dead's gold. It spends the same.

### Omens
The Omen Stone is the castle's oldest stone and it is not quite a stone. Turning it tells the
deep you are coming, and the deep answers:

- **Blood Moon:** the strong among the dead grow stronger, and richer.
- **Famine:** the light in you mends less, and the anvil pays better for embers.
- **Restless Dead:** more of them wake.
- **Glass Bones:** everything breaks easier, including you.

### The Abyss and the Unsung (built)
Beneath the altar of the Black Cathedral, three seals held a door shut. The Cardinal's hymn was
never for the dead of Cathedra; it was sung so the dead would sleep quietly enough not to wake
the thing the seals held. The first three floors' **champions** each carry a fragment of a seal
without knowing it. Take all three in one descent and the Cathedral's portal does not lead home.
It leads down, into **the Abyss**, where the light fails (the Silence) and **the Unsung** waits:
a colossal ribcage hanging in the dark under a skull with three burning eyes, ringed in orbiting
bone. It has never been sung to. It does not know what a song is for. Its wardens are the Mourners
who stood over the seals, and they move the same way they always have: only when no one is looking.

> *"You broke my seals. Then meet the ones who kept them."*
> *"The morning they waited for. It is mine."*

## Open threads (for later)

These are deliberately unanswered. Each one is a hook for a future floor, boss, quest or
secret:

1. **The hidden floor** (built: see "The Abyss and the Unsung"). What lies below the Abyss?
2. **The first expedition.** The anvil came back up. The people who carried it did not, all of
   them. One of them is the dealer at Deadman's Hand. It has never said which.
3. **The Baron's oath.** If the Gravewarden is the first Baron Ravenhold, who is the Baron now, and
   why does the current Baron never come down to the camp?
4. **The cat.** It goes down the shaft at night. It comes back.
5. **The Last Morning.** Cathedra was certain it would come. It has not. Every guardian, in its
   way, is waiting for it. What happens when the party is the one that brings it?

## How the lore is in the game (built)
- **Camp chatter** (`UI/CampChatter`): the six people above speak over their heads when you are
  near. News comes first and reads what *you* have done, on this machine and this run:
  guardians felled, Seals carried, the cat's verdict, journal pages read, wins, parties lost.
  Then idle talk.
- **Deeds** (`Progression/Deeds`): which guardians you have felled, whether you have seen the
  Abyss, and how many runs you have won or lost, kept across sessions. The camp remembers them.
- **Last words**: every guardian says something as it falls (in its own voice; see each boss).
- **Eulogies**: the first time you fell each guardian, its eulogy is written into your Journal
  (Esc > Guide > Journal > Eulogies).
- **Journal pages** on lecterns, one per floor (twelve to find).
- **The descent** shows a line of the Cardinal's hymn under "INTO THE DEEP".
- **Epitaphs**: the run summary ends with the camp's one-line verdict on your descent ("Here lies
  ..., who picked up the cat without asking. The smell outlived them.").
